# Ethics, safety, and misuse risks

## Intended use

Constructive Alignment Auditor is a curriculum-review aid for research, prototyping, and instructional-design work. It surfaces transparent signals that can help a person decide what to inspect more closely.

## Human oversight

A reviewer should be able to see the detected verbs, inferred levels, level gaps, lexical overlaps, and review flags. The system should support discussion rather than replace disciplinary and pedagogical judgment.

## Main misuse risk

The largest risk is treating a simple rule baseline as an objective measure of course quality. Bloom verbs and word overlap cannot determine whether a curriculum is educationally sound.

Do not use the prototype to automatically:

- approve or reject programmes
- make accreditation decisions
- rank instructors or departments
- evaluate employee performance
- grade students
- infer teaching competence

## Context and fairness

Disciplines use language differently. A verb that appears low or high in a generic taxonomy may function differently in mathematics, engineering, design, medicine, humanities, or professional education. Review error patterns across disciplines and educational levels rather than assuming one lexicon transfers uniformly.

## Privacy and governance

Curriculum documents may contain confidential institutional material even when they contain no learner data. Use only documents that may lawfully be processed, retain only what the study needs, and do not publish restricted programme or accreditation materials without permission.

## Before a real study

Document:

- who supplied the curriculum material and under what permission
- reviewer expertise and conflicts of interest
- annotation instructions
- disagreement-handling procedures
- how automated flags are shown to reviewers
- whether seeing the automated output changes reviewer judgment
- rollback procedures if the tool creates misleading review pressure
